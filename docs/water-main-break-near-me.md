# Water Off or Flooding the Street? Water Main Breaks Explained

**Sudden low pressure plus water surfacing anywhere it shouldn't is a main
break — call the utility's emergency line and don't assume someone else has,
because utilities locate breaks from the calls they get.** Here's the
diagnosis path, what to do inside your house while it's out, and the honest
translation of a boil-water advisory.

<div class="tldr" markdown="1">
<span class="tldr__label">Key takeaways</span>
**Diagnose:** neighbors dry too = main/system; just you = your line or
valve. **Report:** utility **emergency line** (24/7); 911 too if a road or
structure is threatened. **Protect the house:** don't run hot water while
pressure's off; flush the **cold tap nearest the meter** when it returns.
**Boil-water advisory** = rolling boil 1 min for anything you swallow, until
officially lifted. **Hydrant blasting on a calm day** = scheduled flushing,
not a break. **The dispatch angle:** breaks, closures, and flushing all run
over [public-works and dispatch radio](#kit).
</div>

## The two-minute diagnosis

1. **Cold tap dead or trickling?** Ask one neighbor. Both dry → system
   problem; just you → your shutoff valves, meter valve, or service line.
2. **Look and listen outside:** water in the gutter on a dry day, a wet
   sinkhole forming, the hiss of pressurized water — walk the block before
   you call, because "water visible at [address]" beats "my water's off"
   for dispatching a crew.
3. **Check the utility's alerts page/social feed** for known breaks and
   planned work; then **call the emergency number anyway** if your street
   isn't listed — outage maps for water are far worse than
   [electric ones](/power-outage-near-me/), and calls are the sensor.
4. **Escalate to 911 only when** water is undermining pavement (cars +
   voids under asphalt end badly), entering buildings, or anywhere near
   electrical equipment.

## Inside the house: while it's off, and when it returns

- **While it's off:** avoid running hot taps (a draining water heater can
  burn out its elements), skip the dishwasher/laundry, and keep a filled
  kettle's worth for hand-washing. Toilets: one bucket of any water poured
  into the bowl flushes it.
- **When pressure returns:** expect air spitting and
  [brown water](/whats-burning-near-me/) — sediment, not sewage. Run the
  **cold** tap nearest the meter until clear, then the rest; clean aerators
  if flow stays poor; hold laundry until water runs clear (iron stains set
  permanently).
- **Under a boil-water advisory:** rolling boil for one minute for
  drinking, cooking, ice, coffee, and teeth; bottled works too. Shower with
  mouth closed; sanitize-cycle dishwashers are fine. Dump ice made during
  the advisory. It lifts after consecutive clean samples — usually a day or
  two, announced the same ways it was issued.

## The false alarm that fools everyone: hydrant flushing

A hydrant roaring into the street with a bored utility worker beside it is
**scheduled flushing** — deliberate scouring of the mains that also causes
the pressure dip and brown-water burst that make half the neighborhood
think "break." Utilities post schedules, and the work is coordinated over
**public-works radio**, which is exactly how scanner listeners settle
