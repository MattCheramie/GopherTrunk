# Power Outage Near Me: What Happened and When Is It Back?

**The two answers you want — what happened and when it's coming back — live in
two different places: the cause goes out over public-safety and utility radio
within minutes, while the restoration estimate lives on your utility's outage
map and gets real once a crew is on scene.** Here's how to work both, including
when the outage has taken your Wi-Fi with it.

## First five minutes

1. **Check the outage map** on phone data and **report your address** — the
   map is built from reports and smart-meter pings, and micro-outages that
   nobody reports sit at the back of the queue.
2. **Look and listen:** a bang beforehand points at a transformer or fuse; a
   storm points at trees; total silence with half the street lit points at a
   circuit fault. **Wires down or anything arcing → 911, keep everyone far
   away** — treat every line as live.
3. **Scan the radio picture:** wires-down and pole-hit calls are dispatched
   to fire and police immediately — often naming your exact cause and cross
   street — and utility crews coordinate on their own channels. A
   [county feed](/listen-to-police-scanner-online/) has the public-safety
   side; the outage's cause is frequently spoken there long before the map's
   "cause: under investigation" updates.

## Reading the restoration tea leaves

The map's first estimate is a placeholder. It firms up when a crew is
**assigned**, and the truth is visible from your porch: an assessment truck
driving the line, then a bucket crew at the fault. On the radio you'll hear
the sequence directly — fire clearing the scene, the utility taking over,
re-energizing. Storm nights run on triage: big circuits, hospitals, and
schools first, single transformers last. Your quiet cul-de-sac's best lever
is that report you filed in minute one.
