## First, the honest truth about this budget

Digital police (P25) scanners start around **$380**. That is simply the floor for
a standalone radio that decodes the modes most metro agencies now use. So under
$100, a *dedicated* radio can only do **analog**. The one way to hear **digital**
for under $100 is to skip the dedicated radio entirely and use a
**[$30 RTL-SDR](/reference/rtl-sdr/)** dongle with free
[GopherTrunk](/downloads.html) on a PC you already own.

> **Check your agencies before you spend anything.** Look up your county on
> [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/). If police, fire, and EMS are
> **analog**, a $100 handheld is genuinely all you need. If they're **digital
> P25**, an analog handheld will hear only unintelligible noise — go the SDR route.

## Why the SDR wins on value (with a catch)

For the price of a nice lunch, a [$30 RTL-SDR](/reference/rtl-sdr/) plus
[GopherTrunk](/downloads.html) does something **no** analog handheld at any price
can: decode **digital** P25, DMR, and NXDN. It also records, timestamps, and logs
every call and gives you a web console. The catch is honest — it needs a
**computer** and a bit of setup, so it's not grab-and-go like a handheld. That
trade is the whole story of [scanner vs SDR](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/).

## The encryption wall (same at every price)

To be crystal clear: **nothing** under $100 — or over it — decodes
**AES-encrypted** police. Not a $100 handheld, not a $30 SDR, not a $650 flagship.
It's a [cryptographic and legal wall](/police-scanner-encryption/). If your city
encrypted, spend your budget on fire, EMS, and neighboring agencies still in the
clear rather than chasing a decode that doesn't exist.
