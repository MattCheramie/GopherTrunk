# Why Is There a Police Checkpoint or Roadblock Ahead?

**The setup tells you what it is before anyone says a word: a lit, signed,
coned operation working every car briefly is a checkpoint; a hasty line of
cruisers with officers looking *into* cars is a perimeter; cones diverting
everyone away is a closure — and each one asks something different of
you.** Here's how to read them, what happens at each, and how to find out
what tonight's is about.

## Reading the roadblock from five cars back

- **DUI / sobriety checkpoint** — formal: advance signs, generator lights,
  a marked pattern (every car, or every Nth). Evenings, weekends,
  holidays. Expect a 20–60 second contact. Legal in most states, banned
  in about a dozen by their own constitutions; frequently announced in
  advance — a quick search for your city + "checkpoint" often finds the
  press release with times.
- **Perimeter / manhunt** — improvised: cruisers angled across lanes,
  officers with flashlights checking back seats and truck beds of cars
  *leaving* an area, often a
  [helicopter working overhead](/helicopter-circling-my-neighborhood/).
  It's about who's hiding, not about you; it collapses when the search
  does.
- **Scene diversion** — everyone turned, nobody questioned: the road ahead
  belongs to a [crash](/accident-near-me/), fire, or investigation. The
  [closure playbook](/road-closed-near-me/) covers duration tells.
- **Commercial inspection detail** — trucks waved into a lot, cars waved
  through. DOT and state police weigh/inspection blitzes; not your event
  unless you're in a truck.
- **Event traffic control** — cones plus a calendar
  ([parade, race, motorcade](/road-closed-near-me/)); posted in advance.

## At the window: the two-minute version

Slow early and follow hand signals; kill high beams; window down, interior
light on at night if you want the fastest possible contact. At a formal
checkpoint have your license reachable — not retrieved at speed while
approaching. Answer the brief questions or don't (states vary on what's
compellable beyond documents), but the window is not where procedural
debates are won; comply, note, and contest later if you believe something
was wrong. Recording is lawful; obstructing isn't. And the one that
matters at 1 a.m.: **a legal turn before a checkpoint is generally fine;
an illegal or erratic one is the probable cause you just donated.**
