## The over-the-road radio stack

A working truck cab usually has two radios doing two different jobs:

- **CB radio (27 MHz)** — *two-way.* This is how drivers warn each other about
  crashes, open or closed weigh stations, smokey ahead, and detours. It's a
  conversation, not a scanner.
- **Scanner** — *receive-only.* Highway patrol dispatch, DOT/weigh-station nets,
  road maintenance, and local police as you roll through town. The
  [BearTracker BCT15X](/reference/uniden-bearcat-bct15x/) is the one purpose-built
  for this on the move.

You want both. A CB gives you the driver grapevine; a scanner gives you the
official traffic the grapevine is reacting to.

## Powering and mounting in the cab

- **Power:** wire the scanner to switched 12V or run a cigarette/USB adapter lead.
  A base/mobile unit expects 12V, not AA cells.
- **Antenna:** the single biggest upgrade. A roof- or mirror-mount external
  antenna dramatically outperforms any rubber duck at highway speed.
- **Placement:** dash-mount within reach and eyeline, but don't block airbags or
  controls.

## What about digital and recording?

you passed through, drop a **[$30 RTL-SDR](/reference/rtl-sdr/)** and free

and NXDN in software and timestamps every call, so you can review the day's
traffic at the truck stop. It's not a dash radio — you trade instant, hands-free
operation for recording and digital reach. Our
