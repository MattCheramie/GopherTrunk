# Best Police Scanner for New York (2026)

**New York is the toughest RF environment on this list, and that pushes the recommendation toward a True I/Q radio in the metros — while much of upstate stays simple.** New York City and its boroughs, plus Buffalo, Rochester, Syracuse and Albany, run digital [P25](/reference/project-25/) with closely spaced transmitters and heavy multipath from dense construction; some systems are [simulcast](/reference/simulcast/). Upstate and rural counties still carry conventional analog. Check your county on [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) first.

## Why New York City punishes cheap scanners

Dense urban RF is a worst case for scanning: tall buildings reflect signals, transmitters sit close together, and [P25 Phase II](/reference/simulcast/) [simulcast](/reference/simulcast/) piles overlapping copies of the same channel on top of that. Budget radios smear it into digital noise. The Uniden [SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) and [SDS200](/reference/uniden-sds200/) use a True I/Q front end that handles multipath and simulcast far better, which is why they're the standard pick for New York City and the other dense metros.

> **Upstate is a different world.** If your county is not simulcast — common outside the big cities — a [BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) or [BCD996P2](/reference/uniden-bcd996p2/) gives the same P25 audio without the premium.

## Beginners and upstate counties

New to it? The [Uniden BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) handheld programs from your ZIP code and loads local systems from its database — full P25, no manual frequency tables. For a base station, the BCD536HP is the desktop version. Both are far easier than manually programming a scanner for New York's many overlapping systems.

## Rural New York: analog still lives

Across the Adirondacks, the Southern Tier and rural upstate, fire and public-works traffic is often still conventional analog. If RadioReference shows analog for your county, a ~$110 [Uniden BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/) covers it plus aircraft, marine, rail and NOAA weather. No need for a digital radio in an analog county.

## The free alternative: RTL-SDR + GopherTrunk

With a PC, a **~$30 RTL-SDR dongle running free [GopherTrunk](/downloads.html)** decodes New York P25, DMR and NXDN in software, follows unlimited talkgroups at once, and records and timestamps every call to a web console — useful when you want to catch multiple boroughs' talkgroups simultaneously. It needs a computer and isn't pocket-portable; see [scanner vs SDR](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/).

## Encryption is a real factor in New York

Encryption has been spreading among some New York agencies. **No consumer scanner and no SDR — GopherTrunk included — can legally decode encrypted P25.** It is a cryptographic and legal wall, not something you can buy around. This matters more here than in many states, so **before you spend a dollar, confirm your specific agency is still in the clear** on [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) and read the [encryption explainer](/police-scanner-encryption/).

## Bottom line

For New York City and the dense metros, the **[Uniden SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) or [SDS200](/reference/uniden-sds200/)** is the best police scanner because urban multipath and simulcast defeat cheaper radios. Upstate and in non-simulcast counties, a **[BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/)** matches the audio for less, and an analog **[BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/)** covers rural areas. Because **[encryption](/police-scanner-encryption/) is spreading and undecodable on any device**, check your agency first — and if you own a PC, a **[$30 RTL-SDR + GopherTrunk](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/)** is worth trying before buying.
