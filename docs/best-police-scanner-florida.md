# Best Police Scanner for Florida (2026)

**In Florida the scanner question splits two ways — which P25 system covers your county, and whether you need a battery handheld that keeps working when a hurricane takes out the grid.** Miami, Tampa, Orlando and Jacksonville run large digital [P25](/reference/project-25/) systems, several of them [simulcast](/reference/simulcast/), and Florida operates a statewide P25 law-enforcement radio system that many agencies join. Coverage varies, so check your county on [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) before buying.

## Why the Florida metros need True I/Q

The largest Florida systems are [P25](/reference/project-25/), and several metro systems are [P25 Phase II](/reference/simulcast/) simulcast — multiple towers keying one frequency at once, which is what garbles cheaper scanners. The Uniden [SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) and [SDS200](/reference/uniden-sds200/) use a True I/Q front end built to demodulate simulcast cleanly, and that is why they're the standard recommendation for Miami-Dade, the Tampa Bay area, Orlando and Jacksonville.

> **Simulcast is the reason for the price.** If your county isn't simulcast, a [BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) or [BCD996P2](/reference/uniden-bcd996p2/) gives identical P25 audio for less. Confirm on RadioReference before you upgrade.

## Hurricane season: why hardware earns its keep

Florida is the one state where the *form factor* matters as much as the mode. A battery-powered handheld — the [SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) or [BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) — keeps monitoring public-safety, EMS and NOAA weather traffic when the power and internet are down. That off-grid reliability is the clearest case where a self-contained scanner beats a PC-based setup that depends on mains power.

## Beginners and smaller counties

If you're new, the [Uniden BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) programs from your ZIP code and loads local systems automatically — full P25, no frequency tables. In smaller Florida counties where fire or public-works channels are still conventional analog, a ~$110 [Uniden BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/) covers them plus aircraft, marine and weather. Don't buy digital for an analog county — match the radio to what RadioReference actually lists for your area, not to the biggest metro in the state.

## The free alternative: RTL-SDR + GopherTrunk

If you have a PC, a **~$30 RTL-SDR dongle running free [GopherTrunk](/downloads.html)** decodes Florida P25, DMR and NXDN in software, follows unlimited talkgroups, and records and timestamps every call. The catch during storm season: it needs a computer and mains power, so a battery handheld is the safer off-grid tool. Compare them in [scanner vs SDR](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/).

## Encryption in Florida

Some Florida agencies encrypt part or all of their talkgroups. **No scanner and no SDR — including GopherTrunk — can legally decode encrypted P25.** It's a cryptographic and legal wall. Check your specific agency on [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) before buying and read the [encryption explainer](/police-scanner-encryption/).
