# Smell Smoke? How to Find Out What's Burning Near You

**"Smoke investigation" is one of the most common calls in the fire service —
so common that the fastest way to find out what's burning near you is usually
to listen to the crews being sent to investigate the same smell you're
standing in.** Here's the lookup order, from your own house (rule that out
first) to a wildfire three states away.

<div class="tldr" markdown="1">
<span class="tldr__label">Key takeaways</span>
**Inside smell / burning plastic:** check your own home; persistent unexplained
smell = **911**. **Neighborhood smell:** county fire dispatch on a
[scanner feed](/listen-to-police-scanner-online/) or [your own scanner](#kit) —
"smoke investigation" calls answer it live. **Regional campfire smell / haze:**
**fire.airnow.gov** (smoke + AQI), **Watch Duty** (western US), state
forestry burn schedules. **Column of smoke growing:** call 911 — early reports
matter.
</div>

## First: rule out your own house

A smoke smell **indoors** with no obvious source gets treated as an emergency
until proven otherwise: check each room, sniff at outlets and appliances
(burning plastic or hot electrical smell = breaker off, 911 on). Fire crews
run these calls constantly with thermal cameras precisely because smoldering
wiring hides in walls. Never talk yourself out of that call.

## Local: the "smoke investigation" channel

When the smell or haze is outdoors and local, your fire department is
probably already rolling on it — someone else called too. On your county's
fire dispatch you'll hear:

- **"Smoke investigation" / "odor of smoke"** calls with the block they're
  sent to — often your own.
- **The finding:** an illegal burn, a barbecue, a vehicle fire, a mulch fire,
  a neighbor's fireplace, or a working fire getting a full assignment.
- **Fire traffic stays listenable:** fire/EMS channels
  [usually remain unencrypted](/police-scanner-encryption/) even where police
  encrypt, making this the most reliably open corner of public-safety radio.

A [Broadcastify feed](/listen-to-police-scanner-online/) carries it if one
exists for your county; the [fire & EMS scanner guide](/fire-ems-scanner/)
and the [kit below](#kit) make it permanent. Seeing trucks but no smoke?
That's its own explainer: [fire trucks, but no fire](/fire-trucks-near-me/).

## Regional: wildfire and prescribed-burn smoke

A campfire smell across the whole area with hazy sun usually isn't local at
all:

- **[fire.airnow.gov](https://fire.airnow.gov/)** — the definitive map:
  active fires, modeled smoke plumes, and your real-time air quality (AQI).
- **Watch Duty** — the best wildfire app for the western US: fires, evacuation
  zones, and updates from actual fire-radio listeners.
- **InciWeb + state forestry pages** — official large-incident details and
  **prescribed-burn schedules**, the most common "mystery smoke" answer in
  spring and fall.
- **Winter note:** still, cold nights trap neighborhood wood smoke under an
  inversion — everyone smells "fire" because fifty fireplaces are lit.

## When to stop looking things up and call 911

A **visible, growing smoke column**, flames anywhere, or that indoor
electrical smell — call. Early smoke reports are how small fires stay small,
and dispatchers universally prefer a false alarm to a late one. Everything
else on this page is for the 95% of smoke smells that have a boring
explanation — and for hearing the fire service confirm it, live, while your
neighbors are still typing "does anyone else smell smoke??" into
[Nextdoor](/sirens-near-me/).
