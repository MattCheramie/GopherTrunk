## The travel problem: you keep moving into new coverage

A home scanner is programmed once. On a road trip you might pass through six states
in a day, each with its own [trunked systems](/reference/trunked-radio/) and
patrol frequencies. You can't stop and reprogram at every county line — so the
right travel scanner either **alerts you automatically** or **re-loads your
location in seconds** from a nationwide database.

## BearTracker highway-alert mode, explained

The BCT15X's headline travel feature is its **BearTracker Warning System**. Instead
of you knowing every state's patrol frequencies, it watches known state-police and
DOT [trunked systems](/reference/trunked-radio/) in the background and **alerts you
when there's activity from an agency near you**. It's the closest thing to a
"radios ahead" warning built into a scanner. Note it targets the analog and
conventional patrol traffic common on interstates — where an agency runs encrypted
digital, no scanner will hear it.

## Multi-state coverage from one database

The [BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) and
[HomePatrol-2](/reference/uniden-home-patrol-2/) carry the **HomePatrol database**
— the entire United States, preloaded. When you reach a new metro, you re-run the
location search (ZIP or GPS) and it swaps in that area's police, fire, EMS, and
[talkgroups](/reference/talkgroup/). No internet, no laptop, no per-state frequency
hunting. For a deeper look at what those systems carry, see our
[scanner frequency guide](/scanner-frequencies/).

## Powering a scanner in the car

- **Mobile (BCT15X):** wire to switched 12V, or run a cigarette-lighter adapter for
  a quick install in a rental or borrowed vehicle.
- **Handheld (BCD436HP):** charge from any car USB port — keep a spare cable in the
  glovebox and it never runs flat.
- **Antenna matters more than power.** A cheap mag-mount on the roof will out-hear
  the rubber-duck antenna every time on the open highway.
