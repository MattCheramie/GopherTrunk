# Best Police Scanner for Washington (2026)

**Washington divides into two scanning worlds: dense Puget Sound digital and the
mountain-and-farmland east.**
Many agencies run [Project 25](/reference/project-25/) digital trunked systems, and
across the Seattle–Tacoma–Everett corridor those are frequently
[P25 Phase II simulcast](/reference/simulcast/) on large regional networks — while
eastern Washington and the rural counties range from P25 to conventional analog.
Before you buy, look up your county on [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/)
and note whether your local sites are **simulcast P25** or **plain analog** — that,
plus the Cascades and Puget Sound terrain, decides your scanner.

## Match the scanner to your part of Washington

| Where you are | Likely system | Buy this |
|---|---|---|
| Seattle, Tacoma, Everett, Bellevue (King/Pierce/Snohomish) | P25 Phase II, heavy simulcast | [SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) / SDS200 |
| Spokane, Vancouver, Olympia, Bellingham | P25, some simulcast | [SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) or [BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) |

> **Terrain is half the battle here.** Between Puget Sound simulcast overlap and the
> hills and water that block signals, antenna and location matter as much as the
> radio. [Simulcast](/reference/simulcast/) is where the
> [True I/Q SDS](/reference/uniden-sds100/) wins; in the mountains a good outdoor
> antenna often matters more than the receiver. Confirm your sites on RadioReference
> first.

## New to scanning

The [Uniden BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) (handheld) programs from your ZIP
code and loads the right talkgroups automatically — no frequency tables, full P25
Phase I/II. Where your sites aren't simulcast it sounds identical to an SDS for well
over $100 less; in the dense Puget Sound simulcast core it can struggle where the SDS
won't. Match it to what RadioReference shows for your county.

## Still-analog Washington

Rural eastern Washington, some fire districts, and business users stay on conventional
analog. Where your targets are analog, skip digital: a ~$110
[Uniden BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/) covers VHF/UHF plus marine — genuinely
useful along the Sound and the coast — as well as air and rail. See the
[main buyer's guide](/best-police-scanners/) for more.

## The free Washington alternative: SDR + GopherTrunk

Own a computer? Skip the hardware. A **~$30 [RTL-SDR](/downloads.html) dongle** plus
free, open-source **GopherTrunk** decodes P25 (Phase I and II), DMR, NXDN and TETRA in
software. It records and timestamps **every** call, follows unlimited talkgroups at
once, logs radio IDs, and streams to a browser console. The trade-off is a PC and
antenna, and no pocket portability. Read the honest
[scanner vs SDR comparison](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/) and grab the software from
[downloads](/downloads.html).

> **The encryption wall.** No SDS, no BCD436HP, and no SDR decodes
> **[AES encryption](/police-scanner-encryption/)**. Where a Washington agency
> encrypts, it's silent for everyone. Buy for what's still in the clear.

## Bottom line

If you're anywhere around Puget Sound, Washington's P25 systems are commonly **Phase

scanner that actually decodes them through the region's tough terrain. In the
non-simulcast towns a **[BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/)** does the same for
less, and across still-analog eastern Washington a
**[BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/)** with a good antenna is all you need. Have a
