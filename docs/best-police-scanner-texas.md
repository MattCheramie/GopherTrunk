# Best Police Scanner for Texas (2026)

**Texas is big enough that the right scanner in Houston is the wrong scanner in the Panhandle — the deciding factor is P25 Phase II simulcast in the metros versus conventional analog out in the country.** Houston, Dallas–Fort Worth, San Antonio, Austin and El Paso run large digital [P25](/reference/project-25/) systems, and the state has statewide interoperability infrastructure that many local agencies participate in. Rural West Texas and the Panhandle still carry conventional analog. Check your county on [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) before you buy.

## Why Texas metros want True I/Q

Several of the largest Texas radio systems are [P25 Phase II](/reference/simulcast/), and a number use [simulcast](/reference/simulcast/) — many towers transmitting the same channel at once. That overlapping signal is the single hardest thing for a scanner to decode, and it is where budget radios fall apart. The Uniden [SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) and [SDS200](/reference/uniden-sds200/) were built around a True I/Q front end specifically to demodulate simulcast, which is why they are the default recommendation for the Houston, DFW, San Antonio and Austin metros.

> **Not every Texas county is simulcast.** If yours isn't, a [BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) or [BCD996P2](/reference/uniden-bcd996p2/) gives you the same P25 audio without paying the simulcast premium. Verify on RadioReference before spending the extra $130.

## Beginners: start from your ZIP code

New to scanning? The [Uniden BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) handheld (or the BCD536HP base) programs from your Texas ZIP code and pulls the local systems from its onboard database. No manual frequency entry, full P25 Phase I/II. It is the least frustrating way to get a first digital scanner working.

## Rural West Texas and the Panhandle

Out past the metros, a lot of Texas fire, EMS and public-works traffic is still conventional analog. If RadioReference lists your county as analog, don't overspend — a ~$110 [Uniden BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/) handles it and adds aircraft, marine, rail and NOAA weather. Only step up to a digital radio when your agencies actually go digital.

## The free alternative: RTL-SDR + GopherTrunk

Own a PC? The cheapest way into Texas P25 is a **~$30 RTL-SDR dongle running free [GopherTrunk](/downloads.html)**. It decodes P25, DMR and NXDN in software, tracks unlimited talkgroups simultaneously, and records and timestamps every call to a browser console. The trade-off is that it needs a computer and isn't pocket-portable — see [scanner vs SDR](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/).

## Encryption in Texas

Some Texas agencies encrypt part or all of their talkgroups. **No scanner and no SDR — GopherTrunk included — can legally decode encrypted P25.** That is a cryptographic and legal barrier, full stop. Before buying, confirm your agency is still unencrypted on [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) and read the [encryption explainer](/police-scanner-encryption/).

## Bottom line

For the Texas metros — Houston, Dallas–Fort Worth, San Antonio and Austin — the **[Uniden SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) or [SDS200](/reference/uniden-sds200/)** is the best police scanner because those systems are P25 simulcast. Away from simulcast, a **[BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/)** matches the audio for less, and an analog **[BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/)** covers rural counties cheaply.
