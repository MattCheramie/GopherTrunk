*Part 7 of **Squelch**, a 14-part deep dive into GopherTrunk's conventional
scanner — the scan list with no control channel to announce a call — and
every gate that decides when a channel opens.
[Part 6]({{ '/blog/deep-dives/squelch-06-dcs/' | relative_url }}) finished
the FM tone gates, where a discriminator is the natural instrument. This
part leaves FM. The VHF air band is AM, and on an AM channel the
discriminator hears nothing, the noise squelch of Part 3 has nothing to
measure, and the carrier is never quite where the channel was tuned
([#1219](https://github.com/MattCheramie/GopherTrunk/issues/1219)).*

> **TL;DR:** `mode: am` on a scan-list channel changes two things. The
> squelch becomes `amCNMeter` (`am_squelch.go`): decimate to ~48 kHz,
> Welch-average eight 256-point Hann spectra (~188 Hz bins, ~43 ms), and
> divide the strongest bin within ±4.5 kHz by the 20th-percentile bin within
> ±12 kHz. Noise reads 2.6–7 dB at any level; the channel opens at
> `squelch_cn_db` (default 12) and `squelch_dbfs` is ignored. The call is
> granted as `am-conv` and runs the FM chain with `am=true`: `amCarrierAFC`
> mixes the carrier to DC, then a ±4.5 kHz filter, `demod.AM` (envelope ÷
> tracked carrier − 1), a 3 kHz low-pass and `amAudioScale` 0.4 — no
> de-emphasis, no CMA. The first on-air captures carried the carrier at
> −3315 Hz and lost a sideband until the chain tracked it. On-air verified
> 29 Sep.

**Key takeaways**

- **AM has no quieting, so the FM answer does not transfer.** The
  scale-free statistic for AM is the carrier line over the channel's own
  noise floor.
- **A ratio of two things that scale together cannot be moved by gain.**
  Peak bin and percentile floor both follow the gain knob; their quotient
  does not.
- **The carrier is the reference twice.** The squelch measures it, and the
  detector divides by it, so recorded level follows modulation depth and
  never signal strength.
- **Tuning error is a filter problem before it is an audio problem.** A
  carrier 3.3 kHz off tune stays inside a ±4.5 kHz filter and loses its far
  sideband. Find the carrier first, then filter.

## Why AM needed its own gate

Before #1219 the scan list's analog modes were `fm` and `nfm` — the list
[Operator's Cookbook Part 6]({{ '/blog/tutorials/operator-cookbook-06-analog-fm-tone-out/' | relative_url }})
built — and both rest on the discriminator. An
[AM]({{ '/reference/amplitude-modulation/' | relative_url }}) carrier has
no frequency modulation, so the discriminator turned an air-band
transmission into silence; the comment on the chain's test says the air
band "recorded nothing even where a channel could be tuned". AM needed a
demodulator. It also needed a squelch, and neither existing one fits.

The level squelch of
[Part 2]({{ '/blog/deep-dives/squelch-02-channel-power/' | relative_url }})
is absolute [dBFS]({{ '/blog/tutorials/analog-edge-02-dbfs/' | relative_url }}),
with the weakness the whole series is about. The noise
squelch of
[Part 3]({{ '/blog/deep-dives/squelch-03-noise-squelch/' | relative_url }})
works because an FM carrier *quiets* the discriminator, and an envelope
detector has no such behaviour. The header of `am_squelch.go` states both
constraints in one sentence: "An AM channel cannot use a noise-quieting
statistic (AM has no capture effect), and the rule for every new gate here
is that it must not depend on absolute dBFS (a gain change would silently
move it)."

What AM does offer is its carrier. An AM transmitter radiates it for as
long as it is keyed — "a spectral line holding at least half the signal
power" — while receiver noise is flat across the channel. A line against a
flat floor is a ratio, and a ratio is what
[The Analog Edge Part 13]({{ '/blog/tutorials/analog-edge-13-coherence-not-dbfs/' | relative_url }})
asks for: never gate on absolute dBFS when a scale-invariant statistic
exists.

## Measuring a carrier against its own floor

`amCNMeter` decimates the scanner's IQ to about 48 kHz and keeps a
[Welch-averaged]({{ '/reference/welch-method/' | relative_url }}) power
spectrum: 256-point Hann-windowed blocks (187.5 Hz bins) summed over a ring
of the last eight. The first reading exists after eight blocks, about 43 ms;
after that every new block updates it. Two sets of bins were picked once at
construction, and the statistic is one division:

```go
// internal/scanner/conventional/am_squelch.go (shape)
var peak float64
for _, k := range a.carrier { // bins within ±4.5 kHz of the tuned frequency
	peak = math.Max(peak, a.sum[k])
}
for i, k := range a.floor { // bins within ±12 kHz
	a.sorted[i] = a.sum[k]
}
sort.Float64s(a.sorted)
floor := a.sorted[int(amCNFloorPercentile*float64(len(a.sorted)))] // 0.2
a.level = 10 * math.Log10(peak/floor)
```

Each choice has a stated reason. The peak is searched within ±4.5 kHz
because "air-band transmitters and the SDR's own ppm error leave it within
a few kHz, and ±4.5 kHz stays clear of an 8.33 kHz neighbour's carrier".
The floor is a *percentile*, not a mean, which "keeps the floor honest when
the voice sidebands or an 8.33/25 kHz neighbour occupy part of the view".
And because peak and floor scale together with gain, the result is a
[carrier-to-noise ratio]({{ '/reference/carrier-to-noise-ratio/' | relative_url }})
in one ~188 Hz bin, in dB, with no reference to full scale.

On noise alone the reading is not zero: it is the largest of about 48
averaged noise bins over a low percentile of them, a few dB. The project's
engineering notes put it at 2.6–7 dB at any level, with a carrier tracking
its true C/N to within 1–2 dB. `DefaultAMSquelchCNDb` is 12, the dwell
applies the same 3 dB hysteresis as every other channel, so a call holds
down to 9 dB, and `amMinDwell` (100 ms) keeps every scan window long enough
for the first average. Two tests pin the level independence from opposite
directions.
`TestAMCNMeterNoiseStaysClosed` feeds ten seconds of noise at two levels
60 dB apart and requires the reading never reach even the 9 dB close level.
`TestAMSquelchIgnoresAbsoluteLevel` builds the two cases a level squelch
gets backwards: the channel must open on a 25 dB carrier at −85 dBFS, 35 dB
under `squelch_dbfs`, and stay shut on receiver noise at −17 dBFS, 33 dB
over it.

To the operator all of this is one key:

```yaml
scanner:
  conventional:
    - label: "Tower"
      frequency_hz: 118700000
      mode: am
      squelch_cn_db: 12    # default 12 (noise alone reads ~3–7 dB)
```

Inside the scanner it is one function. `squelchMeasure` returns a level
function and an open threshold — `am.process` and `SquelchCNDb` on an AM
channel, in-channel power and `SquelchDbFS` otherwise — and the scan window
and the dwell use them without knowing which they hold. An open logs
`conv: squelch opened` with `level_cn_db` and `squelch_cn_db`. The full rig
is
[Cookbook Vol. 2 Part 1]({{ '/blog/tutorials/cookbook-volume-2-01-airband-rig/' | relative_url }}).

## The same chain, with five differences

An AM dwell is granted with protocol `am-conv`, which the composer maps to
`voiceKindAM` and runs as `runFMChain` with its `am` flag set. Decimation to
a 48 kHz intermediate rate, the audio high-pass, resampling, the
squelch-tail mute and PCM delivery are shared with FM. Five things differ.

1. **A carrier tracker runs first** — the next section.
2. **The channel filter is fixed at ±4.5 kHz** (`amChannelCutoffHz`, 181
   Kaiser taps). Air-band voice is
   [double-sideband]({{ '/reference/double-sideband/' | relative_url }})
   with about 3 kHz of audio, and ±4.5 kHz keeps it "while rejecting an
   8.33 kHz neighbour, whose nearest sideband starts ~5.3 kHz away".
3. **The detector is `demod.AM`**, a carrier-referenced envelope detector.
4. **The audio is low-passed at 3 kHz and scaled by `amAudioScale`** = 0.4,
   "so an AM channel records at the level of a ±3 kHz NFM one".
5. **Two FM stages are skipped**: de-emphasis, because "AM is not
   pre-emphasised", and the CMA equalizer, whose "constant-modulus target
   is wrong for AM, whose envelope IS the audio".

The detector is three lines:

```go
// internal/dsp/demod/am.go (shape)
env := math.Hypot(float64(real(s)), float64(imag(s)))
a.carrier += a.alpha * (env - a.carrier) // 100 ms one-pole average
m := env/a.carrier - 1                   // modulation depth, clamped to ±1.5
```

Dividing the envelope by a slow average of itself removes the carrier and
sets the gain in one step, so, as its comment says, "the output level
depends on how deeply the transmitter is modulated, never on how strong it
arrives" — the carrier is the AGC reference an FM chain has to add after
the fact. `TestComposerAMChainRecoversAirBandAudio` runs two carriers 40 dB
apart and requires the recorded 1 kHz tone to match within 10 %, with an
equal-strength neighbour 8.33 kHz up ending more than 40 dB under it.

## The carrier that was not where it was tuned

The first on-air captures arrived on 28 Sep, made with the reporter's own
aircraft radio, with an observation attached: the channel "tunes best at
123.453, not 123.450". That reads like a frequency error in GopherTrunk. It
was not, and the capture says so directly —
[reading the capture first]({{ '/blog/solution-postmortem/rebuilt-from-the-air-13-read-the-capture-first/' | relative_url }})
again. The file is the per-call voice IQ debug tap — the exact samples the
chain decoded, with the scanner's LO offset already mixed out — and in it
the carrier sits at **−3315 Hz** relative to 123.453 MHz. The transmitter,
as that SDR sees it, is at about 123.4497 MHz. 123.450 was right all along.

What the offset exposed was a real defect one stage later. The channel
filter was centred on the *tuned* frequency. A carrier 3.3 kHz off stays
inside ±4.5 kHz, but its far sideband does not: the header of `am_afc.go`
works it out as "the lower sideband is cut above ~1.2 kHz of audio and the
envelope detector sees carrier + one sideband (half the audio level, plus
distortion)". Whatever made 123.453 sound better by ear, the capture shows
a chain that was throwing away part of the signal.

`amCarrierAFC` fixes the cause. Every 2048 samples at the intermediate rate
(about 23 Hz bins, a new estimate every ~43 ms) it finds the strongest bin
within ±4.5 kHz — deliberately the squelch's window, "so the squelch and
the chain agree on what 'the channel' is". The bin steers the mixer only if
it stands 15 dB over the median of that window; on the #1219 captures a
carrier stands 30–55 dB over it and receiver noise about 10 dB at most.
A parabolic fit refines the peak, the first estimate is taken outright and
later ones are smoothed, and with no line present the tracker holds its
last value (`TestAMCarrierAFCHoldsOnNoise`). The carrier is then mixed to
DC *before* the channel filter. On the two captures the correlation between
as-captured and recentred audio went from 0.954 and 0.886 to 1.000 and
0.999 (`TestAMCaptureReplay`, the `GT_AM_IQ` harness). When the tracked
offset reaches 1 kHz the chain says so at call end:
`composer: AM carrier found off the tuned frequency; tracked and recentred,
but set the channel's frequency_hz (or sdr.ppm) closer to it`.

## What pins it, and how not to measure it

On 29 Sep, on a build with the tracking change (#1227), the reporter heard
"very good and clear" audio, on frequency, with no added noise or
artifacts. AM on the scan list is **on-air verified**.

That run also left the fixtures. The reporter's 126.400 MHz capture (a
departure frequency at KRIC, per the test's comment) has the transmitter
keyed for its first second and a half and receiver noise after it unkeys —
carrier and noise from one receiver at one gain. Two 48 kHz
[cs16]({{ '/blog/tutorials/analog-edge-10-capture-discipline/' | relative_url }})
slices are committed as `testdata/am_kric_126400k_carrier_48k.cs16` and
`am_kric_126400k_noise_48k.cs16`, and `TestAMSquelchOnRealAirCarrier`
replays them at the scanner's 2.4 MS/s: the carrier slice must stay above
12 dB throughout (it reads 47–61 dB) and the noise slice must never reach
9 dB (it reads 2.7–6.1 dB).

The same slice pins the tracker, and taught a lesson about measuring.
`TestAMChainRealAirTuningErrorKeepsAudio` shifts the carrier 3315 Hz off
centre and compares the decode with the on-centre one. The obvious metric,
audio correlation, is useless here: about 84 % of that capture's audio is
hum below 300 Hz from the reporter's hangar, so the chain *without*
tracking still correlated 0.995 while losing 4.1 dB between 1 and 3 kHz.
The test measures power in the 1–3 kHz band and demands agreement within
1 dB — a small cousin of
[metrics that lie]({{ '/blog/deep-dives/weak-signal-engineering-02-metrics-that-lie/' | relative_url }}).
In production the default 300 Hz `fm_audio_highpass_hz` applies to AM too;
the test harness runs without it.

Two fixture traps are recorded with the tests. The chain's default PCM
decimation restarts its phase on every IQ chunk, so a coherent tone
measurement needs the polyphase `AudioResampler` enabled. And the
counterfactual "the FM chain cannot hear AM" must use a lone carrier,
because two carriers in one discriminator beat and the beat carries their
envelopes.

What is not done: the 127-tap 3 kHz audio low-pass has a skirt about 2 kHz
wide (−2.4 dB at 2.5 kHz) and was left alone, and an ACARS burst on an AM
channel also opens an ordinary AM call —
[Part 11]({{ '/blog/deep-dives/squelch-11-data-on-the-scan-list/' | relative_url }}).

### How AM shaped the Go code

- **A flag, not a fork.** `runFMChain` takes `am bool`; the AM-only stages
  are nil on an FM call and the shared ones are untouched.
- **One squelch call site.** `squelchMeasure` hides which statistic a
  channel uses behind a function and a threshold.
- **Squelch and chain share a definition of the channel.**
  `amCNCarrierHalfHz` and `amAFCSearchHz` are both 4 500 Hz, and the second
  constant's comment says it matches the first.
- **Real-air fixtures cross package lines.** The composer's tracking test
  reads the scanner's KRIC slice rather than keep a copy.

## FAQ

**How does GopherTrunk squelch an AM air-band channel?**
It measures the carrier-to-noise ratio from the channel's own spectrum: the
strongest bin within ±4.5 kHz of the tuned frequency divided by the
20th-percentile bin within ±12 kHz, over eight averaged 256-point blocks.
The channel opens at `squelch_cn_db`, 12 dB by default, and holds to 3 dB
below it.

**Why is squelch_dbfs ignored on an AM channel?**
Because an absolute level moves with tuner gain and a ratio does not. Noise
reads 2.6–7 dB on the AM meter at any level, so one threshold works across
gain settings and front ends; a test opens the channel on a carrier at
−85 dBFS and keeps it shut on noise at −17 dBFS.

**What happens if an AM channel is tuned a few kilohertz off frequency?**
The voice chain finds the carrier within ±4.5 kHz of the tuned frequency
and mixes it to zero before the channel filter, so both sidebands pass. If
the tracked offset reaches 1 kHz it logs the offset at call end and
suggests correcting `frequency_hz` or `sdr.ppm`.

**Does signal strength change how loud an AM recording is?**
No. The detector divides the envelope by a 100 ms average of the carrier,
so its output is modulation depth. A test runs two carriers 40 dB apart and
requires the recorded tone level to match within 10 %.

**Is AM on the scan list verified on air?**
Yes — on-air verified on 29 Sep, when the reporter heard clear, on-frequency
audio. Two real-air slices of a 126.400 MHz transmission and the noise
after it are committed as regression fixtures for the squelch and the
carrier tracking.
