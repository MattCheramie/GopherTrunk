## Why fire & EMS stay in the clear

Police encryption gets the headlines, but fire and EMS have strong reasons to
remain open, and mostly do.

- **Interoperability and mutual aid.** A working fire pulls in neighboring
  departments, county resources, and sometimes multiple states. Encryption breaks
  the cross-agency coordination fire relies on, so plain audio wins.
- **Fewer privacy pressures.** Much of what drives police encryption — broadcasting
  personal data, criminal-history queries — doesn't apply the same way to fire
  suppression and rescue.
- **Hospital coordination is open by design.** EMS-to-hospital medical traffic on
  the MED channels is meant to be simple and widely receivable.

> **The practical upshot.** When someone says "my county encrypted, scanning is
> dead," fire and EMS are usually the answer that keeps it alive. Verify per-agency
> on [RadioReference](/reference/radioreference/) — but the odds are good.

## Where fire & EMS live on the dial

Fire and EMS span several bands. The audio most listeners want — the on-scene
tactical traffic — is usually VHF simplex.

| Traffic | Typical band | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fire dispatch** | VHF high, UHF, 700/800 trunked | Tone-outs and unit assignments |
| **Fireground / tactical** | VHF high (~151–159 MHz) | On-scene simplex — the good stuff |
| **EMS dispatch** | VHF, UHF, 700/800 trunked | Ambulance calls |
| **EMS MED channels** | ~155 MHz VHF + 462/467 UHF MED | Ambulance ↔ hospital, usually analog/clear |
| **Legacy rural/volunteer** | VHF low/high | Often still analog — cheap to scan |

See the full band plan on [scanner frequencies by service](/scanner-frequencies/).

## Monitoring medical and ambulance traffic

The MED channels are a distinct, rewarding target — ambulance crews calling
reports to receiving hospitals.

- **VHF MED channels** sit around 155 MHz on many systems, and the **UHF MED
  channels** near 462/467 MHz handle med-control patches. Both are typically
  analog and unencrypted.
- **EMS dispatch** may be trunked while the **MED patch** stays conventional and
  clear — so an analog scanner can catch hospital coordination even where dispatch
  is digital.
- **Fireground rehab and EMS on scene** often share VHF tactical channels at a
  working incident, so a fire scan naturally sweeps up the medical side too.

## The free alternative

If you own a PC, a ~$30 [RTL-SDR](/reference/rtl-sdr/) running free
[GopherTrunk](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/) decodes the same P25/DMR/NXDN fire and EMS
systems — and **records and timestamps every call**, so you can replay the tone-out
you missed. It won't fit in your pocket like a BCD436HP, but for a home fire/EMS
monitor logging 24/7, it's hard to beat. Fire and EMS are exactly the kind of
clear, high-activity traffic that makes an always-on GopherTrunk box worthwhile.
