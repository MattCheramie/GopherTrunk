## Start with an honest question: is your PD still open?

Before you spend a dollar, look up your city, county, and fire district on
[RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) and check whether dispatch is
**unencrypted**. This matters more than any hardware spec:

- Many departments still run **open analog or unencrypted P25** dispatch — a
  capable scanner hears all of it.
- A **growing number** have moved dispatch to **full AES encryption**. When that
  happens, **no scanner and no SDR can decode it** — it's a cryptographic and
  legal wall ([18 U.S.C. §2512](/police-scanner-encryption/)). Any product
  claiming to "unlock" encrypted police is a scam.

> **If your city encrypted, don't give up — pivot.** Fire and EMS are encrypted
> far less often than police, and public-works, utility, mutual-aid, and
> neighboring-agency traffic frequently stays in the clear. A watch group can get
> real situational awareness from those even when the PD itself has gone dark.
> See our [fire & EMS scanner guide](/fire-ems-scanner/).

## Why a base station beats a handheld at home

For a fixed spot at home, a **base/mobile** unit on a rooftop or attic antenna
buries any handheld sitting indoors on its stock antenna. You get more range, a
bigger speaker, mains power that never dies, and — on the
[BCD536HP](/reference/uniden-bcd536hp/) — Wi-Fi streaming so the whole household
can listen from a phone.

If anyone in the watch group is nervous about menus, the

## Want a record, not just a live feed?

A neighborhood watch often wants to *review* what happened overnight, not just
hear it live. A **[$30 RTL-SDR](/reference/rtl-sdr/)** running free
[GopherTrunk](/downloads.html) on any home PC decodes P25/DMR/NXDN in software and

rather than a replacement — see [scanner vs SDR](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/). It still
