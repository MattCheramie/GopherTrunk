**MPT 1327** is an analog [trunked-radio](/reference/trunked-radio/) signalling standard
that originated in the **United Kingdom**. It uses a **dedicated
[control channel](/reference/control-channel/)** carrying **1200 bit/s
[FFSK](/reference/ffsk/)** data to set up and manage analog FM voice channels, and it became
one of the most widely adopted open trunking standards internationally.[^wiki] Because the
signalling was published openly rather than tied to one manufacturer, MPT 1327 equipment was
built by many vendors and deployed across dozens of countries.

## Overview

MPT 1327 specifies the **control-channel signalling** — call requests, channel grants,
short data messages, and unit/group identities — while the actual voice remains analog FM on
the channels the control channel assigns. Signalling uses **FFSK** (fast frequency-shift
keying, a form of audio FSK) at 1200 bit/s, chosen so it can pass through ordinary FM voice
equipment. The standard is one of a family of related UK Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications
(MPT) documents: MPT 1327 defines the signalling protocol, **MPT 1343** the mobile numbering
and registration scheme common in Europe, and **MPT 1347** the radio interface requirements.
Identities are structured as prefix, fleet, and unit numbers, and the system supports group
calls, individual calls, status messages, and short data. Its openness made it the natural
choice for operators who wanted trunking without committing to a single vendor's ecosystem.

## History

MPT 1327 was **published in the UK in the 1980s** by the then British telecommunications
regulator as an open specification for trunked private mobile radio.[^wiki][^rr] It filled the
role that proprietary systems like [Motorola Type II](/reference/motorola-type-ii/) and
[EDACS](/reference/edacs/) played elsewhere, but with a multi-vendor, standards-based approach.
Widespread adoption followed across Europe and beyond, and the MPT family remained the dominant
open analog trunking standard until digital systems such as DMR and TETRA arrived.

## Deployment

MPT 1327 saw heavy use in **business, transport, and government fleets** across Europe, Asia,
Africa, Australasia, and South America — taxi networks, utilities, and national operators
among them. It is now **declining** as digital standards take over, but a substantial installed
base persists, especially in regions where the systems remain cost-effective for existing users.

## Decoding it with GopherTrunk

Following an MPT 1327 system means demodulating the **1200 bit/s FFSK control channel**,
decoding its address and grant messages, and steering the receiver to the assigned analog FM
voice channel — the standard control-channel trunk-tracking workflow. Because voice is plain
FM, no vocoder is involved. See the [Status](/status.html) page for GopherTrunk's current
MPT 1327 control-channel support.
