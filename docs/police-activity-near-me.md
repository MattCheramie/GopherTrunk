# Police Activity Near Me: How to Find Out What's Going On

**When there are police cars on your street and no explanation, the answer is
almost always sitting on the dispatch radio — and in most of the US you can
legally listen to it live.** Everything else (apps, crime maps, news, asking an
officer) is either delayed, curated, or limited to major incidents. Here is
every source, fastest first, with what each one actually covers.

## Live: hear the dispatch traffic itself

Police work is coordinated by radio. The call type, the address, the units
assigned, the arrival, the outcome — it's all broadcast, and where dispatch is
unencrypted it's [legal to listen](/police-scanner-legal/).

- **[Broadcastify](https://www.broadcastify.com/)** (browser) and **Scanner
  Radio / 5-0 Radio** (phone) stream volunteer-run feeds by county.
- Listen for your street name; dispatchers give block-level addresses.
- No feed for your county, or the wrong channels relayed? That's the gap
  running [your own scanner](/how-police-scanners-work/) closes.

## Near-live: CAD call logs and incident apps

- **Active calls-for-service pages.** Search `"<county> sheriff active
  calls"` / `"<city> police calls for service"`. These are generated straight
  from the dispatch computer, so they include the routine calls nothing else
  reports — often with a short intentional delay, and sensitive call types
  filtered.
- **Citizen** curates scanner traffic into map alerts, but only in select
  major metros.
- **PulsePoint** covers the fire/EMS side where agencies opt in — useful
  because a medical call also explains flashing lights on a quiet street.

## After the fact: crime maps, blotters, and records

If the activity was earlier today or last night:

- **Crime maps** — [CityProtect](https://www.cityprotect.com/),
  [Community Crime Map](https://communitycrimemap.com/),
  [CrimeMapping.com](https://www.crimemapping.com/), and SpotCrime plot
  reported incidents by address once reports post (hours to days later).
- **The blotter / press releases** on the agency site or local paper.
- **A public-records request** for the incident report — every state has a
  process; expect redactions on open investigations.
- **Your own call history** — a scanner that records, like
  [GopherTrunk](/getting-started.html), timestamps every call so you can
  scroll back to 2 a.m. and press play. That workflow is covered in
  [what happened near me last night](/what-happened-near-me-last-night/).

## Reading the scene while you wait

None of this is a substitute for information, but response patterns do carry
signal: many units arriving fast and blocking the street is an active
incident; one unit parked for an hour is paperwork, radar, or a directed
patrol; units arriving quietly without lights suggests a search or perimeter
— and a [helicopter overhead](/helicopter-circling-my-neighborhood/) usually
confirms it. If you're only *hearing* it, the [siren
sound itself](/what-do-siren-sounds-mean/) narrows down who is responding.

## If it's directly outside your house

When the activity is on *your* stretch of street:

- **Stay inside and watch from cover, not the porch.** If anything about the
  scene looks volatile, put an interior wall between you and it — most calls
  are mundane, but you can't tell which from the driveway.
- **Follow any officer instruction immediately** — "back inside, please" is
  not a conversation opener. You can sort out questions later.
- **Recording from your own property is lawful**; interfering, approaching,
  or lighting up the scene with a flashlight is not smart or welcome. Keep
  distance and doorways clear.
- **If officers set a perimeter that includes your home**, expect either a
  knock (evacuate briefly) or an instruction to stay inside — comply first;
  details come after. The [SWAT-scale version](/swat-team-in-my-neighborhood/)
  has its own page.
- **Afterward:** lock cars and outbuildings if the call was a fleeing
  suspect — post-search neighborhoods see door-handle checks — and note the
  time in case you want to [look the call up later](/what-happened-near-me-last-night/).

## What Nextdoor won't tell you

Neighborhood apps and Facebook groups are where the question gets asked, not
where it gets answered. The thread fills with speculation until someone with a
scanner posts the actual call. Be that neighbor: a
[~$30 RTL-SDR running free GopherTrunk](/cheap-sdr-scanner/) decodes digital
P25/DMR/NXDN dispatch and records everything, or a
[ZIP-code-programmable scanner](/best-scanner-for-neighborhood-watch/) does it
with zero setup. Start with [how police scanners
work](/how-police-scanners-work/) — and check
[RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) first to confirm your
agency's dispatch is still [in the clear](/police-scanner-encryption/).
