# What Was That Loud Boom? How to Find Out

**A boom big enough to make you search for it made other people call 911 —
and within about two minutes a unit is dispatched to "check the area," which
means the fastest answer is hearing what that unit finds.** Here's the
suspect list, the fast lookups, and how to tell the harmless booms from the
call-911 ones.

<div class="tldr" markdown="1">
<span class="tldr__label">Key takeaways</span>
**Boom + power flicker** = transformer/fuse fault → utility outage map
([power outage near me](/power-outage-near-me/)). **Boom + ground shake** =
check USGS "Did You Feel It?" — quakes, blasting, and sonic booms all show
up there. **Boom + nothing** = fireworks, exploding targets, blasting, or a
[gunshot you're unsure about](/gunshots-or-fireworks/). **The verdict:** the
"area check" dispatch and its outcome, live on a
[scanner feed](/listen-to-police-scanner-online/) or [your own
scanner](#kit). **Gas smell, smoke, or damage → 911.**
</div>

## The suspect list

- **Transformer / fuse fault** — gunshot-to-cannon bang, often a flash and a
  block losing power. The [outage-map check](/power-outage-near-me/) confirms
  it in one look.
- **Fireworks mortars & exploding targets** — single deep booms from a
  distance, evenings and weekends, rural echo. Kin to the
  [gunshots-or-fireworks question](/gunshots-or-fireworks/).
- **Quarry / construction blasting** — weekday, daytime, often felt as a
  thump then heard; permitted and scheduled, frequently the answer to
  "recurring 11 a.m. boom."
- **Vehicle strike** — a car into a building, pole, or dumpster sounds
  exactly like an explosion one street over; this one *will* draw
  [sirens](/sirens-near-me/).
- **Sonic boom** — military supersonic flight or a spacecraft reentry;
  rattles windows across tens of miles. Pairs with
  [military jets overhead](/military-jets-flying-over-my-house/).
- **Frost quake (cryoseism)** — the coldest-night special: ground cracking
  with a rifle-crack boom and a very local shake.
- **Earthquake / gas explosion** — the two you rule out first when there's
  shaking plus damage or a gas smell: USGS for the first, 911 for the second.

## The five-minute lookup

1. **Utility outage map** — did a block just go dark? Transformer. Done.
2. **USGS "Did You Feel It?"** ([earthquake.usgs.gov](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/))
   — earthquakes post within minutes, and sonic-boom reports cluster there
   too, tagged as non-earthquakes.
3. **The dispatch radio** — booms generate 911 calls; a
   [county feed](/listen-to-police-scanner-online/) carries the "loud
   explosion reported, checking the area" run and, crucially, the outcome:
   "fireworks debris located," "transformer down," or "nothing found."
4. **Local social media** — a wide-area boom lights up town groups across
   several ZIP codes in minutes; purely local posts point at something on
   your block.
5. **Next morning:** if you slept on it, [reconstruct
   it](/what-happened-near-me-last-night/) from the CAD log and feed
   archives.

## When a boom is a 911 call

A gas smell, smoke, debris, damage, or people hurt — call, immediately.
Explosions with consequences are exactly what the fire service wants early
reports on. Everything else on this page is for the boom that leaves nothing
behind but your curiosity — which is the usual kind, and the kind the radio
answers while everyone else is still posting question marks.
