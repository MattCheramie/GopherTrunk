# Why Is a Drone Flying Over My House? How to Identify It

**Since 2024, nearly every drone in US airspace is required to broadcast its
identity and its pilot's location — which means a free phone app can usually
tell you whose drone that is while it's still overhead.** Here's the likely
owner list, the identification method, what's legal on both sides of the
fence, and the one thing you must never do about it.

<div class="tldr" markdown="1">
<span class="tldr__label">Key takeaways</span>
**Identify it:** a free **Remote ID app** (Bluetooth/Wi-Fi) shows the
drone's ID, altitude, and **where the pilot is standing**. **Night drone
with a spotlight working yards** = likely a police search — the ground side
is on [dispatch](/police-activity-near-me/). **Legal reality:** overflight
is lawful (FAA airspace); harassment/peeping is not (state law) — document
+ non-emergency line. **Never** shoot at or jam a drone — both are federal
offenses. **Also check** a flight tracker: "drones" at night are often
[manned aircraft](/helicopter-circling-my-neighborhood/) at a distance.

## Who it probably is

- **A neighbor/hobbyist** — the most common answer; short flights near home,
  often FPV practice at dusk.
- **A working drone** — roof/real-estate/insurance shots or survey mapping:
  methodical grid or orbit patterns over one property or block, daytime.
- **Utility inspection** — lines and towers, flying the corridor like the
  [helicopter patrols](/helicopter-circling-my-neighborhood/) do.
- **A police drone** — patrol, crash scene, or a Drone-as-First-Responder
  launch; at night with a light, working backyards, it's the drone version
  of a search helicopter, and the ground units coordinating with it are on
  [dispatch](/police-activity-near-me/).
- **Not a drone at all** — hovering lights at night are frequently a
  helicopter, plane, planet, or satellite. One flight-tracker check
  ([how](/helicopter-circling-my-neighborhood/)) settles it.

## Identify it while it's still up there

1. **Open a Remote ID app** (search your app store for "Remote ID" /
   "Drone Scanner" — they read the OpenDroneID broadcast over Bluetooth and
   Wi-Fi). Nearly all drones flying legally today must broadcast.
2. **Read the result:** drone ID, altitude, speed — and the **operator's
   position**, usually a person standing within line of sight. Question
   answered, most of the time.
3. **No Remote ID showing?** Either it's a toy-class drone under the weight
   threshold, a rule-breaker, or it isn't a drone — check the
   [flight tracker](/helicopter-circling-my-neighborhood/) next.
4. **Police-drone hunch?** The launch and the call it's flying to are often
   audible on the agency's [dispatch](/listen-to-police-scanner-online/)
   where unencrypted; some DFR programs even publish flight logs.

## The legal lines, both directions

The FAA owns the airspace from the grass up — routine overflight of your
property is lawful, and you have no right to interdict it: **shooting at a
drone is a federal crime, and jamming is illegal, period.** What the law
*does* protect you from is behavior: persistent hovering to surveil,
harassment, peeping — that's state privacy/harassment law. The effective
response is documentation (video, dates/times, the Remote ID readout showing
the operator location) and the **police non-emergency line**; identifying a
registered operator from there is straightforward.

## Sightings worth reporting — and to whom

Most drones are boring. These aren't, and each has a correct destination:

- **Near an airport or under a final-approach path** → the airport's
  operations line or the FAA's regional office; this is the sighting the
  system most wants.
- **Over a wildfire or police air operation** → 911, immediately: one hobby
  drone grounds every firefighting aircraft on the incident ("If you fly,
  we can't").
- **Over crowds, stadiums, or school events** → on-site security or the
  police non-emergency line.
- **Reckless flying or repeated harassment/peeping** → police non-emergency
  with your video, times, and Remote ID readout; persistent cases escalate
  through state harassment/voyeurism law.
- **911 is for immediate danger only** — a drone merely annoying you is a
  non-emergency call, and "I shot at it" turns *you* into the case.
