# Why Is the Road Closed? Find Out What's Blocking Traffic

**A road closure is a radio operation — every barricade, detour, and re-opening
is coordinated over police dispatch, so the "why" is broadcast in plain language
the whole time the road is shut.** Apps will show you *that* it's closed;
here's how to find out *why*, and how long you're actually going to be stuck.

<div class="tldr" markdown="1">
<span class="tldr__label">Key takeaways</span>
**The reason is on dispatch:** a county [scanner feed](/listen-to-police-scanner-online/)
or [your own scanner](#kit) hears the closure being run. **Highways:** the
state **511/DOT map** posts cause + duration estimates. **Planned closures:**
city public-works / police social accounts. **Duration tells:** crash 30–90
min; wires down = utility ETA; fatal investigation = hours; gas leak =
open-ended. **Don't** call 911 to ask.
</div>

## The fast lookups

1. **Scanner feed** — [Broadcastify](/listen-to-police-scanner-online/) for
   your county. You'll hear the incident itself plus the practical gold: which
   cross streets are shut, where traffic is being turned, and the "open it
   back up" call the moment it comes.
2. **State 511 / DOT map** — search `"<state> 511"`. Authoritative for
   highways: cause, lanes, and a clearance estimate.
3. **Waze / Google Maps** — instant closure geometry, useful for routing
   around, silent on cause.
4. **City / agency social feeds** — planned closures (parades, races,
   utility work, filming) are posted in advance; search your city plus "road
   closures."

## Decoding what you see at the barricade

- **Fire trucks + utility trucks** → wires, gas, or water: the road opens
  when the utility clears it, not before. ([Why fire trucks with no
  fire?](/fire-trucks-near-me/))
- **Many police units, measuring wheels, cameras** → serious-crash
  investigation; plan on hours. ([Accident lookup](/accident-near-me/))
- **Tape a block over, officers relaxed at the barricade** → the scene is
  elsewhere; you're at the perimeter of a
  [police operation](/police-activity-near-me/).
- **A helicopter on or near the roadway** → medevac landing zone; total but
  brief closure. ([Identify it](/helicopter-circling-my-neighborhood/))
- **Cones and a calendar** → planned work or an event; the city posted it
  somewhere last week.

## The commuter's cheat code

People who drive the same corridor daily eventually notice the pattern:
traffic apps react to a closure *after* everyone is already stopped, while
the closure went out over the radio *before* the first barricade was set. A
scanner in the car — or a [GopherTrunk box](#kit) at home feeding your phone
on the way out the door — moves you from the reacting crowd to the informed
one. Related picks: the [road-trip scanner guide](/best-scanner-for-road-trips/)
and the [trucker scanner guide](/best-scanner-for-truckers/), both built
around exactly this use.
