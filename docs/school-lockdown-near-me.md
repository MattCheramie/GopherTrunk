# Why Is the School on Lockdown? What Parents Should Do

**Most school lockdowns are precautionary — police activity in the
neighborhood, not a threat inside — and the single most important thing a
parent can do is also the hardest one: don't drive to the school.** Here's
what the terminology actually means, how the next hour will unfold, and how
to get real information while the official messages catch up.

<div class="tldr" markdown="1">
<span class="tldr__label">Key takeaways</span>
**"Secure" ≠ "Lockdown":** secure = threat *outside*, doors locked, class
continues (routine); lockdown = threat at/inside the building (rare, often a
hoax). **Do not go to the school** — you'll block the response; wait for the
**reunification** message and bring photo ID. **Text once**, calmly; don't
call repeatedly. **Info while you wait:** district + PD social feeds, county
[alerts you registered for](/emergency-alert-just-went-off/), and — where
unencrypted — the [dispatch traffic itself](#kit), where the all-clear
happens first.
</div>

## Decode the word the school used

Most districts follow the **Standard Response Protocol**, and the exact term
in the message tells you the shape of the event:

- **Hold** — hallways cleared, class continues; usually a medical event or
  maintenance issue *inside*. Lowest concern.
- **Secure** (older term: "lockout") — something is happening *outside*:
  a [police search nearby](/police-activity-near-me/), a
  [pursuit](/sirens-near-me/), a bank robbery a mile away. Exterior doors
  lock, recess comes inside, teaching continues. This is by far the most
  common event, and it usually ends within the hour when the outside
  incident resolves.
- **Lockdown** — a threat at or inside the building: locked doors, lights
  out, students out of sight. Rare — and a painful share of the dramatic
  ones are **swatting hoaxes**, fake calls engineered to draw exactly the
  massive response you're seeing.
- **Evacuate / Shelter** — movement orders; the school's message will name
  where.

## The next hour, from the outside

1. **The first message is thin on purpose** — staff secure kids first,
   communicate second. A vague first text is procedure, not concealment.
2. **The response looks huge regardless** — agencies surge on every school
   call and stand down after the canvass. Many units ≠ confirmed threat.
3. **Reunification, if needed, happens off-site** — a named location,
   controlled release, photo ID required. This is why driving to the school
   accomplishes nothing: your child won't be released at the front door.
4. **The all-clear** lands as a message and, before that, as radio traffic —
   "building canvass complete, code 4" — which is why scanner-listening
   parents are calm twenty minutes early.

## What to do — and not do — while it runs

- **One text, calm, short.** "I know what's happening. I love you. Follow
  your teacher." Then stop — silent classrooms full of buzzing phones and
  crying return-texts are a genuine operational problem.
- **Stay off the school's phone lines** — the office is running the
  protocol, and 911 does not have school updates for you.
- **Don't spread screenshots.** Mid-incident rumors (and hoax calls
  themselves) travel through parent group chats faster than any fact;
  forward the school's messages, not the speculation.
- **Registered for [county and district alerts](/emergency-alert-just-went-off/)?**
  This event is why. Do it today if not.
