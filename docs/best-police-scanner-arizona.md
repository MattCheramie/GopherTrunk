# Best Police Scanner for Arizona (2026)

**Arizona splits sharply between dense metro P25 and vast, thinly-covered desert.**
Many metro agencies run [Project 25](/reference/project-25/) digital trunked systems,
and around the Phoenix Valley those are frequently
[P25 Phase II simulcast](/reference/simulcast/) across large multi-site networks —
while the desert, mountains, and tribal lands range from P25 to plain analog. Before
you buy, look up your county on [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) and
note whether your local sites are **simulcast P25** or **conventional analog** — that,
plus Arizona's wide-open geography, decides your scanner.

## Match the scanner to your part of Arizona

| Where you are | Likely system | Buy this |
|---|---|---|
| Phoenix Valley (Phoenix, Mesa, Scottsdale, Tempe) | P25 Phase II, heavy simulcast | [SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) / SDS200 |
| Tucson, Flagstaff, Yuma | P25, some simulcast | [SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) or [BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) |
| Smaller towns, non-simulcast P25 | P25 Phase I/II | [BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) / [BCD996P2](/reference/uniden-bcd996p2/) |
| Rural desert, mountains & tribal areas | Mixed P25 / conventional analog | [BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/) |

> **Geography matters in Arizona.** Between the Phoenix simulcast overlap zones and the
> huge distances to rural repeaters, antenna and location matter as much as the radio.
> [Simulcast](/reference/simulcast/) is where the [True I/Q SDS](/reference/uniden-sds100/)
> earns its keep; out in the desert a good outdoor antenna often matters more than the
> receiver. Confirm your sites on RadioReference first.

## New to scanning

The [Uniden BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) (handheld) programs from your ZIP
code — it loads the right talkgroups automatically, covers P25 Phase I/II, and its
battery portability fits Arizona's outdoor lifestyle. Where your sites aren't
simulcast it sounds identical to an SDS for well over $100 less; in the heavy Phoenix
simulcast core it can struggle where the SDS won't. Match it to what RadioReference
shows for your county.

## Still-analog Arizona

Much of rural Arizona, along with many reservation-area, fire, and business users,
stays on conventional analog. Where your targets are analog, skip digital: a ~$110
[Uniden BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/) covers VHF/UHF plus air (handy near the
state's many general-aviation fields), marine and rail. See the
[main buyer's guide](/best-police-scanners/) for more.

## The free Arizona alternative: SDR + GopherTrunk

Own a computer? Skip the hardware. A **~$30 [RTL-SDR](/downloads.html) dongle** plus
free, open-source **GopherTrunk** decodes P25 (Phase I and II), DMR, NXDN and TETRA in
software. It records and timestamps **every** call, follows unlimited talkgroups at
once, and streams to a browser console you can watch from another room. The trade-off
is a PC and antenna, and no pocket portability. Read the honest
[scanner vs SDR comparison](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/) and get the software from
[downloads](/downloads.html).

> **The encryption wall.** No SDS, no BCD436HP, and no SDR decodes
> **[AES encryption](/police-scanner-encryption/)**. Where an Arizona agency encrypts,
> it's silent for everyone. Buy for what's still in the clear.

## Bottom line

If you're in the Phoenix Valley or Tucson, Arizona's P25 systems are commonly **Phase
II simulcast** and the **[Uniden SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) or SDS200** is the
scanner that actually decodes them. In the non-simulcast towns a
**[BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/)** does the same for less, and across the
still-analog desert and tribal areas a **[BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/)** paired
with a good antenna is all you need. Have a PC? A **[$30 SDR with
GopherTrunk](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/)** records every call for almost nothing.
