# Best Police Scanner for California (2026)

**In California the right police scanner depends almost entirely on whether you live in a P25 Phase II simulcast metro or an inland county still running conventional radio.** Los Angeles, the San Francisco Bay Area, San Diego, Sacramento and San Jose lean heavily on digital [P25](/reference/project-25/) systems — many of them [simulcast](/reference/simulcast/) — while Sierra, far-north and desert counties still carry plenty of analog. Look up your county on [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) first; those facts decide which radio below is right.

## Why the big California metros need a True I/Q radio

California's largest urban radio systems are [P25](/reference/project-25/), and a number of them are [P25 Phase II](/reference/simulcast/) simulcast — several transmitters keying the same frequency at once. That overlapping RF is exactly what turns older scanners into a stream of digital noise. The Uniden [SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) and [SDS200](/reference/uniden-sds200/) use a True I/Q software-defined front end built specifically to demodulate simulcast cleanly, which is why they are the standard recommendation for Los Angeles County, the Bay Area, San Diego and the Sacramento corridor.

> **Simulcast is the whole reason the SDS costs more.** If your county is *not* simulcast, you do not need to pay the premium — a [BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) or [BCD996P2](/reference/uniden-bcd996p2/) decodes the same P25 audio for less.

## Beginners and inland counties

If you are new to scanning, or you live in a suburban or inland county where the digital system is not simulcast, the [Uniden BCD436HP](/reference/uniden-bcd436hp/) is the easiest real digital scanner to own. You enter your ZIP code and it loads the local systems from its database — no manual frequency tables. For a base or in-car unit, the BCD536HP is the desktop equivalent.

## Rural, mountain and desert California

Large stretches of California — the Sierra Nevada, the far north, and the desert east — still run conventional analog for fire, public works and some sheriff traffic. If RadioReference shows your agencies as analog, a ~$110 [Uniden BC125AT](/reference/uniden-bc125at/) covers them, plus aircraft, marine, rail and NOAA weather. Don't overbuy a digital radio for an analog county.

## The free alternative: RTL-SDR + GopherTrunk

If you already own a PC, the cheapest way into California P25 is a **~$30 RTL-SDR dongle running free [GopherTrunk](/downloads.html)**. It decodes P25, DMR and NXDN in software, follows unlimited talkgroups at once, and records and timestamps every call to a web console — something no handheld does. It is not portable and it needs a computer, so read [scanner vs SDR](/police-scanner-vs-sdr/) before deciding.

## A word on encryption

Some California agencies have encrypted some or all of their talkgroups. **No consumer scanner and no SDR — including GopherTrunk — can legally decode encrypted P25.** It is a cryptographic and legal wall, not a hardware limitation you can buy your way past. Before you spend anything, confirm your specific agency is still in the clear on [RadioReference](https://www.radioreference.com/) and read our [encryption explainer](/police-scanner-encryption/).

For most Californians in the LA basin, the Bay Area, San Diego or Sacramento, the **[Uniden SDS100](/reference/uniden-sds100/) or [SDS200](/reference/uniden-sds200/)** is the best police scanner because those regions run P25 simulcast that defeats cheaper radios.
